Q: Record
A: "information or data stored on a medium and used as an extension of human memory or to support accountability" OR "information or data created or received by an organization in the course of its activities; organizational record" —SAA

Q: Provenance
A: The origin of objects, its history of change/transfer, documentation of such.

Q: Records management
A: "The systematic and administrative control of records throughout their life cycle to ensure efficiency and economy in their creation, use, handling, control, maintenance, and disposition." —SAA

Q. OAIS
A: Open Archival Information Systems. Abstract standard, more a conceptual map, of terms in a open archival system with accession, preservation, and dissemination as fundamental supported activities, especially relevant to digital systems. Originally developed in the context of space data, e.g. for intergovernmental cooperation around space stations.

Q. SIP
A. Submission Information Package (OAIS). Submitted data with sufficient information about its contents and structure to add to an archive. Often includes metadata added by archivists, e.g. checksums, manifests.

Q. AIP
A. Archival Information Package (OAIS). Maintained data with sufficient information to support preservation activities like format migration, data integrity (checksums), management. Typically maintained by archivists.

Q. DIP
A. Dissemination Information Package (OAIS). Converted AIP for public consumption, perhaps with less technical metadata but more descriptive metadata.

Q: Life cycle of records
A: Distinct phases of a record's existence, from creation to final disposition, like those of a living organism. Might include stages like: creation, receipt, preservation, use, disposition, destruction/deaccession.

Q: Disposition
A: "Materials' final destruction or transfer to an archives as determined by their appraisal" —SAA.

Q: Deed of Gift
A: Contract/signed document which establishes the archives legal title over donated materials.

Q: Appraisal
A: Process of analyzing materials and determining whether to add them to the archives, often guided by collection policies. "Deciding if you want the thing"—Genna Duplisea, Backlog.

Q: Acquisition
A: "Process of seeking and receiving materials from any source by transfer, donation, or purchase." —SAA. Information capture; get the deed of gift, inventory materials, contextual info from donor/seller.

Q: Accessioning
A: Actually adding material to an archives. SAA: "to take intellectual and physical custody of materials, often under legal or policy authority". Involves creating descriptive metadata, contextualizing within existing collections, archival packaging, and identifying necessary preservation procedures.

Q: MPLP
A: More Product Less Process. Motto from a famous 2005 paper by Mark A. Greene and Dennis Meissner. Archives management philosophy focused on reducing backlogs by processing quickly with less descriptive work. Leads to more collection-level and less item-level metadata.

Q: Reparative description
A: Practice seeking to address historical wrongs insinuated into metadata. Examples involve modernizing the terms used to refer to sexual and ethnic minorities and indigenous peoples.

Q: Digitization
A: Creating digital surrogates of physical materials, e.g. scanned images of printed pages. Often used to increase access to materials but can also be a preservation mechanism.
